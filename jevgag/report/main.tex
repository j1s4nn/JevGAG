\begin{abstract}
Adaptive temporal granularity --- deciding when a causal video generator should produce one frame at a
time (\emph{fine}) versus several frames per chunk (\emph{coarse}) --- rests on four preconditions: a real
mode-dependent cost gap, a pre-generation signal, an endpoint independent of the compared modes, and an
oracle calibrated well enough to gate on. This report audits all four with an off-device,
image-grounded System One oracle (Jev~1.14) on a matched factorial over two accelerators (Tesla T4 and
A100-80GB). We contribute: (i)~a \emph{break-even law} that is conditional on both the hardware and the
operating point, which resolves a confound in the earlier audit of this problem; (ii)~a paired test
showing that an \emph{image}-grounded oracle is a \emph{worse} routing signal than a text-grounded one;
(iii)~a validated independent \emph{endpoint} --- the image-grounded oracle agrees with CLIP subject
consistency at Spearman $\rho{=}{+}1.000$; and (iv)~two honest negatives (a confidence-gated policy that
is neutral, and a stability-gated recalibration that is worse on both GPUs). Every headline number is
re-derived from a stored artifact (18/18 claim-to-artifact checks pass). The reusable output is the
break-even law plus the endpoint; the routing signal remains an open problem.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Causal (autoregressive) video generators can trade computation for quality by changing their
\emph{temporal granularity}: frame-wise generation is accurate but expensive, while chunk-wise generation
is cheaper on some hardware but drifts. A natural system spends fine computation only on hard segments,
deciding before each segment is generated. That system is only viable if four things hold, and this
report measures each of them.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A matched break-even law} (\S\ref{sec:e1}) over
  $\{\text{T4},\text{A100}\}\times\{\text{fine }s_1/s_2/s_4\}\times\{\text{coarse }s_1/s_2/s_4\}$.
  Coarse is cheaper \emph{iff} the measured per-forward ratio falls below the schedule break-even, and
  the same pair can sit on opposite sides of that inequality on the two GPUs. This resolves a confound
  in the earlier audit~\cite{jisan2026audit}, which compared an \emph{expensive} coarse mode on T4 with a
  \emph{cheap} one on A100.
  \item \textbf{A paired modality test} (\S\ref{sec:e2}). On 80 matched segments, an image-grounded
  oracle is a \emph{worse} routing signal than a text-grounded one ($\Delta$AUROC $-0.110$ to $-0.195$).
  \item \textbf{A validated independent endpoint} (\S\ref{sec:e5}). The same image-grounded oracle, used
  as a quality endpoint, agrees with CLIP subject consistency at Spearman $\rho{=}{+}1.000$.
  \item \textbf{Two honest negatives} (\S\ref{sec:e7}, \S\ref{sec:e8}): confidence-gated routing is
  neutral, and stability-gated recalibration is worse on both GPUs.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relationship to the prior study.}
The prior preprint~\cite{jisan2026audit} framed the problem and reported a conditional trade-off on one
GPU. This report is the audit that follows: it measures the preconditions on two GPUs with a matched
design, tests the modality of the oracle, and validates the endpoint. The companion method (DDAG) is a
separate artifact; neither reuses text or figures from the prior preprint.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Causal video generation.}
Streaming generators roll out chunk by chunk, and closing the train--test gap between teacher forcing and
self-rolling is central~\cite{huang2025self,zhu2026causal}. Few-step causal distillation makes the setting
interactive~\cite{zheng2026causalrcm}; we treat such a model as fixed and study its granularity.

\paragraph{Adaptive computation.}
Adaptive computation was introduced for recurrent networks~\cite{graves2016adaptive} and early exiting for
classification~\cite{teerapittayanon2017branchynet,sun2021early}. In generative video, computation is
allocated by step~\cite{le2026dsa}, by chunk shape~\cite{ma2026flexforcing}, by
pruning and routing~\cite{wang2026pare}, and by layer reuse~\cite{adnan2025foresight}. Our object is the
\emph{temporal chunk size} of a fixed backbone, driven by an \emph{off-device} oracle.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Video generators are evaluated along many axes~\cite{huang2024vbench,zheng2025vbench20}; we adopt CLIP
subject consistency~\cite{radford2021clip} and a captioner~\cite{li2022blip} as independent endpoints, and
compare an image-grounded decision-model endpoint against them.

\paragraph{Decision models.}
System One decision models return typed, calibrated answers~\cite{ling2026jev}; independent evidence
suggests pixel-only quality prediction with such models is unreliable~\cite{robitza2026jevqa}, which is
consistent with our signal result.

\section{Preconditions and Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Preconditions.}
\textbf{P1 (cost)} coarse must be cheaper than fine \emph{on the deployment hardware and at the chosen
operating point}. \textbf{P2 (signal)} a quantity computable \emph{before} a segment is generated must
identify coarse-unsafe segments. \textbf{P3 (endpoint)} quality must be certified by a measure
independent of the compared modes. \textbf{P4 (calibration)} the oracle's confidence must be informative
enough to gate on.

\paragraph{Cost model.}
With $t_c,t_f$ the seconds per single forward and $\mathrm{NFE}$ the number of forward evaluations per
segment, coarse is cheaper iff
\begin{equation}
\frac{t_c}{t_f} \;<\; \frac{\mathrm{NFE}_f}{\mathrm{NFE}_c} \;\equiv\; \rho_{\text{be}},
\label{eq:be}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the \emph{measured per-forward ratio} must fall below the \emph{schedule break-even}.

\paragraph{Oracle.}
Jev~1.14 is queried off-device. A \emph{text} state passes a caption plus motion statistics; an
\emph{image} state passes the last three context frames as assets in a \texttt{multipart/form-data}
request with the header \texttt{TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Images:~true}. The oracle returns typed answers
(a difficulty score, a drift risk, a motion class) and a confidence.

\paragraph{Backbone, modes and design.}
Backbone: Causal-rCM Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (commit \texttt{ed3cb14})~\cite{zheng2026causalrcm}. Modes: fine
\texttt{c1-1} ($s$ steps), cheap coarse \texttt{c3-3 s1}, expensive coarse \texttt{c3-3 s4}, and
\texttt{hold} (reuse the previous latent). For every prompt and segment we generate both modes from the
same boundary and the same noise, so the only difference is the generator; a segment is
\emph{coarse-unsafe} when the coarse segment is farther from the four-step fine reference than the fine
segment. Costs are wall-clock DiT seconds with device synchronisation.

\section{Results}

\subsection{E1 --- Break-even is hardware- and operating-point-conditional}
\label{sec:e1}
Table~\ref{tab:e1} reports the matched matrix; Fig.~\ref{fig:e1} visualises it. On T4 the cheap coarse
\texttt{c3-3 s1} beats fine only when fine uses $\ge 2$ steps, and the expensive \texttt{c3-3 s4} is
\emph{never} cheaper; on A100 the cheap coarse beats all three fine modes. The same pair
(\texttt{fine s2}, \texttt{coarse s2}) therefore lies on opposite sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:be} on the two
GPUs ($2.97$ vs.\ break-even $2.68$ on T4; $1.74$ on A100). This is the confound resolved: the prior
audit compared expensive coarse on T4 with cheap coarse on A100.

\subsection{E2 --- Image-grounded Jev does not beat text Jev as a signal}
\label{sec:e2}
On 80 matched segments (Table~\ref{tab:e2}) the image and hybrid states are \emph{worse} than the text
state: paired $\Delta$AUROC of $-0.110$ (difficulty), $-0.108$ (drift) and $-0.195$ (motion), the last
with a 95\,\% CI excluding zero ($P(\text{image}>\text{text})=0.006$). Text-1.13 $\approx$ text-1.14, so
the deficit is the \emph{modality}, not the model version. This is an honest negative and it is
consistent with independent evidence that pixel-only quality prediction with such models is
unreliable~\cite{robitza2026jevqa}.

\subsection{E3 --- The adaptive outcome flips with the operating point alone}
\label{sec:e3}
Same A100, same 12 prompts, only the coarse step differs (Table~\ref{tab:e3}). With the \emph{expensive}
coarse the online policy is forced to 92\,\% fine and cannot beat fixed fine
($4.43$\,s vs.\ $3.81$\,s); with the \emph{cheap} coarse it uses 21\,\% fine and is both cheaper and
closer to the reference ($2.55$\,s vs.\ $3.95$\,s). The adaptive verdict is therefore a property of the
operating point, not of the signal alone.

\subsection{E5 --- Image-grounded Jev is a validated independent endpoint}
\label{sec:e5}
Across six policies and 48 videos, the image-grounded endpoint agrees with CLIP subject
consistency~\cite{radford2021clip} at Spearman $\rho{=}{+}1.000$ (same-subject) and $+0.943$
(consistency); Table~\ref{tab:e5} lists the per-policy values and Fig.~\ref{fig:e5} plots them. This is
the report's strongest positive: the image oracle cannot \emph{drive} the decision (E2) but it can
\emph{certify} it, which is exactly what P3 requires.

\subsection{E6 --- The T4 negative is an operating-point boundary, not a signal failure}
\label{sec:e6}
With fine \texttt{c1-1 s1}, the cheap coarse \texttt{c3-3 s1} saves $-4.57$\,s on T4, i.e.\ it is
\emph{slower}, so adaptive routing cannot save wall-clock time regardless of how good the signal is.
Coarse only wins on T4 once fine uses $\ge 2$ steps ($+7.01$\,s at $s_2$, $+29.70$\,s at $s_4$). This
mirrors E1 and completes the confound explanation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the report establishes.}
The image-grounded oracle is a \emph{poor routing signal} but an \emph{excellent independent endpoint};
the break-even law (Eq.~\eqref{eq:be}) is the reusable engineering tool; and the adaptive verdict is
conditional on hardware \emph{and} operating point, which explains the earlier confound.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i)~\emph{One backbone}: all experiments use a single causal distilled model, so the numbers do not
transfer to other generators. (ii)~\emph{Two accelerators and one schedule family}: T4 and A100-80GB
only. (iii)~\emph{Small samples}: the signal test uses 80 paired segments and the recalibration arms use
$8$--$12$ prompts, so the intervals are wide. (iv)~\emph{Off-device dependency}: the oracle is a versioned
external service (we pin Jev~1.14) and the results depend on its behaviour; the companion method removes
this dependency. (v)~\emph{One modality comparison}: text vs.\ image; other modalities (audio, depth) are
untested. (vi)~\emph{No step-allocation or chunk-shape baseline}: the closest published alternatives
are discussed but not re-implemented.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
The endpoint result (E5) rests on 48 videos from six policies, all on T4; a larger, multi-GPU endpoint
study would strengthen it. The recalibration negatives (E8) are measured on a single seed.

%% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
An off-device, image-grounded System One model can \emph{certify} adaptive temporal granularity even
when it cannot \emph{drive} it, and the cost side obeys a matched break-even law over hardware and
operating point. The routing signal remains the open problem, and we report the two failed policies
openly rather than tuning them to look better.
